# MLX spike, 2026-10-29

Question: would an MLX engine on Apple Silicon beat whisper.cpp (Metal) enough to be worth a second engine? Rule from the phase 5 proposal: adopt only at 30 % faster or more.

Machine: Apple M1 (7-core GPU), 8 GB, macOS 27.0.1. Clips: text-to-speech (Samantha, 175 wpm), 16 kHz mono, generated fresh for this run. Both engines ran large-v3-turbo with English fixed, warm (three runs each after a first run), one after the other with nothing else running.

- whisper.cpp: `whisper-rs` 0.16 (Metal), `ggml-large-v3-turbo-q5_0.bin` (574 MB), flash attention on, the app's `audio_ctx` policy, release build; the engine crate's `spike_timing_on_clips` test.
- MLX: `mlx-whisper` 0.4.3 on `mlx` 0.32.4, `mlx-community/whisper-large-v3-turbo` (fp16, 1.6 GB), Python 3.13, samples passed in as arrays (no ffmpeg).

| Clip | Spoken | whisper.cpp (ms, 3 runs) | MLX (ms, 3 runs) | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| clip_30 | 25.9 s | 2729 · 2717 · 2765 | 2972 · 2851 · 2672 | even |
| clip_10 | 6.7 s | 1123 · 1108 · 1113 | 1740 · 1745 · 1734 | whisper.cpp 36 % faster |
| clip_short | 0.7 s | 993 · 1008 · 1007 | 1543 · 1551 · 1572 | whisper.cpp 35 % faster |

Text was identical on all three clips. First-run costs: whisper.cpp 9.6 s to load (Metal shader compile for a fresh binary; later loads about 0.4 s), MLX 6.4 s including the download of a cached model.

**Decision: not adopted.** MLX matches on a long clip and loses on the short and medium ones, which are the common case for dictation, and the whisper.cpp side already runs the reduced-context trick that MLX does not. A second engine would add a Python or sidecar dependency and 1.6 GB of fp16 weights for no gain on this machine. Revisit if mlx-whisper gains quantised weights and a reduced-context option, or on a machine with a much bigger GPU where the encoder dominates.

Caveats: fp16 against q5_0 quantised weights (the quantised model favours whisper.cpp on memory and may favour it on speed); a single machine; synthetic speech.
